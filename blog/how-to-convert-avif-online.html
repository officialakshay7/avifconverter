How to Convert AVIF Images Online Without Losing Quality

Aug 2026 · 6 min read

Converting an AVIF file to JPG or PNG sounds like it should be simple, and in most cases it is — but there are a handful of details worth understanding if you want to avoid unnecessary quality loss, wasted time, or a tool that quietly stores your images somewhere you didn't expect. This guide walks through the process step by step.

Step 1: Decide which format you actually need

Before converting anything, it helps to know whether you need JPG or PNG, since they serve different purposes. If your AVIF file is a photograph without any transparency, JPG is usually the better choice — it produces smaller files and is supported virtually everywhere. If your file has a transparent background, or you need guaranteed lossless quality for text or fine graphic detail, PNG is the safer option since it preserves transparency and never introduces compression artifacts.

If you're not sure, a quick way to check is to open the AVIF file and look for any areas that should be see-through, like the background behind a logo. If nothing in the image needs transparency, you're almost certainly better off with JPG for the smaller file size.

Step 2: Choose a converter that doesn't upload your files unnecessarily

Plenty of online converters route your images through a server before returning a result — sometimes with a queue, sometimes with a wait for an email link, and occasionally with vague language about how long they retain your uploaded files. That's worth being cautious about, particularly for anything personal, unpublished, or client-confidential.

Look for tools that specifically mention local, in-browser, or client-side conversion. These process the image using your browser's own rendering capabilities rather than sending the file anywhere, which tends to be both faster (no upload or download wait) and more private (nothing to store on a server in the first place). Our own AVIF converter works this way — everything happens in the tab you already have open.

Step 3: Convert your file

The actual conversion process on a well-built tool should be close to instant:

  1. Drag your AVIF file onto the tool, or click to browse and select it.
  2. Choose your output format if the tool supports both JPG and PNG.
  3. If converting to JPG, set the quality level — higher preserves more detail, lower produces a smaller file.
  4. Download the result once conversion finishes.

For a single file, this whole process typically takes just a few seconds from start to finish when the conversion happens locally rather than through a server queue.

Step 4: Check the result before you rely on it

It's worth a quick visual check of your converted file, especially if you're using a lower JPG quality setting or converting an image with fine detail. Zoom in on any areas with text, sharp edges, or subtle gradients, since those are where compression artifacts tend to show up first. If something looks off, try converting again at a higher quality setting — with a local, browser-based tool, this costs you nothing but a few seconds.

Protecting quality during conversion

A few practical tips help minimize any quality loss during the AVIF-to-JPG or AVIF-to-PNG process:

  • Use PNG for graphics and anything with transparency — it's lossless, so there's no compression trade-off to manage at all.
  • Keep JPG quality at 85% or above for anything you plan to print, zoom into, or reuse as a source file later. Lower settings are fine for thumbnails or quick previews where file size matters more than fine detail.
  • Avoid converting the same image back and forth repeatedly between lossy formats. Each lossy re-encode compounds quality loss slightly, so it's best to keep an original copy and always convert from that source rather than from a previously converted file.
  • Match the format to the content — photos to JPG, graphics and transparency to PNG — rather than defaulting to one format for everything.

Batch converting multiple files

If you're dealing with more than a handful of AVIF files — say, an exported photo library or a folder of design assets — look for a converter that explicitly supports batch uploads rather than forcing you to convert one file at a time. A good batch-capable tool lets you drag an entire folder's worth of files onto the page at once, converts each one independently, and offers a way to download everything together rather than clicking through individual downloads one by one.

What to watch out for

A few red flags are worth keeping an eye out for when choosing an online AVIF converter:

  • Vague privacy language. If a tool doesn't clearly explain what happens to your uploaded files, assume the worst and look elsewhere for anything sensitive.
  • Mandatory account creation. A basic image format conversion shouldn't require you to sign up or hand over an email address.
  • Watermarks on the output. Free tools that add a watermark to your converted images are effectively degrading the file for you, which somewhat defeats the point of converting it in the first place.
  • Artificial file count or size limits designed to push you toward a paid tier for what should be a simple, free operation.

Wrapping up

Converting AVIF to JPG or PNG doesn't need to be complicated, slow, or a privacy trade-off. Picking the right output format for your content, using a tool that processes files locally rather than uploading them, and keeping quality settings sensible for your use case covers nearly everything you need to know. From there, it's mostly a matter of dragging your files in and downloading the result — which, with the right tool, takes about as long as it took to read this guide.
