The fastest way to turn AVIF photos into JPG files

JPG is still the most universally recognized image format on the internet, and there's a good reason it hasn't gone anywhere in three decades: almost every device, app, and website knows exactly what to do with it. AVIF is newer, smaller, and technically more efficient, but it's not accepted everywhere yet — which is exactly the moment this AVIF to JPG converter is built for. Drop in your AVIF photo, and you'll have a standard .jpg file ready to upload, email, or print within seconds.

This page is a focused version of our main converter, tuned specifically for JPG output. If you already know you want a JPG rather than a PNG, this is the quicker route — the format is locked in, so there's one less decision to make before you start converting.

Why convert AVIF to JPG specifically

AVIF files are compact and look great in a modern browser, but they run into trouble the moment they leave that environment. Marketplaces like Etsy and Amazon often expect JPG uploads. Older printers and photo labs may not recognize AVIF at all. Some email clients render it as a broken image icon instead of a photo. Even certain versions of design software will simply refuse to open the file. Converting to JPG sidesteps every one of those problems at once, because JPG support is effectively universal.

JPG also happens to be a great structural match for what AVIF photos usually contain: continuous-tone images with lots of natural color variation, like portraits, landscapes, and product photography. Both formats use lossy compression tuned for exactly that kind of content, so converting between them rarely introduces the kind of visible artifacts you'd see converting, say, a screenshot full of sharp text.

How the quality slider actually affects your file

The quality setting controls how aggressively the JPG encoder compresses your image. At the high end (95-100%), you'll get a file that's nearly indistinguishable from the source, at the cost of a larger file size. Dial it down toward 70-80% and the file shrinks noticeably, with a small, usually unnoticeable softening of fine detail — a common sweet spot for web use, where load time matters more than pixel-perfect sharpness. Below about 60%, compression artifacts like blockiness around sharp edges start to become visible, which is worth avoiding for anything you plan to print or zoom into.

There's no universally "correct" setting — it depends on what you're using the image for. A photo going into a printed brochure deserves a higher quality setting than a thumbnail that will be displayed at 200 pixels wide on a product listing. The nice part about converting locally is that you can drag the slider, watch the file size update in real time, and re-download instantly if you want to try a different value — there's no server round-trip slowing that experimentation down.

What happens to transparency

One thing worth knowing before you convert: JPG doesn't support transparency. If your AVIF file has a transparent background — say, it's a logo or a product cutout — converting it to JPG will fill that transparent area with solid white rather than leaving it see-through. For images like that, our AVIF to PNG converter is the better choice, since PNG preserves transparency exactly as it was in the original file. If you're converting a straightforward photograph without any transparent regions, this won't affect you at all.
